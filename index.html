About

I study the economic history of creative markets, using large historical datasets and quantitative methods.

I am a PhD student at the Department of Economics, University of Southern Denmark, and a member of the Historical Economics and Development Group (HEDG). My supervisor is Karol Jan Borowiecki.

My research combines economic history with industrial organization and machine-learning methods. My current work uses the Hofmeister Monatsberichte (1829–1900), the monthly record of new music published in the German-speaking world, to study how publishers and composers responded to copyright law, new printing technology, German unification and rising nationalism. I am part of the research project The Age of Creativity at SDU.

Specialization and Scale in a Creative Market: The Industrial Organization of Nineteenth-Century Music Publishing

with Martin Hørlyk Kristensen, Karol Jan Borowiecki and Marc T. Law
Abstract

How does market structure change as a creative market expands? Using publication records from the Hofmeister Monatsberichte, we follow German music publishers through the nineteenth century and study how copyright, new printing technology and the unification of 1871 changed their scale, specialization and survival, including the shift toward German composers after unification.
